Refining the Model: Controlling for Market Size Bias

In rigorous financial modeling, simple bivariate correlations often suffer from Omitted Variable Bias. A critical confounding factor in our initial observation is Firm Size. Smaller capitalization equities inherently carry higher market risk (Beta) due to lower liquidity, while simultaneously facing different ESG reporting constraints compared to mega-cap peers.

To isolate the true marginal effect of ESG Risk, we expand our methodology to a Multivariate Robust OLS Regression by controlling for Market Capitalization:
Beta ~ overallRisk + ln_mktcap

  • Market Cap Log-Transformation (ln_mktcap): We introduce the natural logarithm of Market Capitalization to control for non-linear size effects and skewness.
  • Robust Standard Errors (HC3): We apply Heteroskedasticity-Consistent standard errors to correct for non-constant variance across the cross-section, ensuring our t-statistics and p-values remain statistically unbiased.

Interpreting the Findings: Does ESG Truly Drive Beta?

The Jump in R² Explanatory Power

In our initial Univariate model, ESG Risk exhibited a weak positive correlation with Market Beta, yielding an R² of merely 3.5%. However, once we introduced Market Capitalization (ln_mktcap) into the Multivariate structure, the adjusted R² surged to 24.5%. This indicates that our second model is structurally superior at explaining the variance in Market Beta across the S&P 500.

Spurious Correlation Revealed

Crucially, upon controlling for size, the coefficient for ESG Risk collapsed and lost all statistical significance (p = 0.413). Conversely, the Firm Size coefficient proved highly significant (p < 0.001). This structural adjustment reveals that the initially observed correlation was largely a Spurious Correlation.

Conclusion: Smaller firms inherently exhibit higher market volatility (Beta) and simultaneously tend to score poorer on ESG metrics due to limited capital resources for sustainability reporting. Therefore, the structural regression confirms that Market Cap is the dominant confounding variable. Unmanaged ESG Risk, in isolation, does not reliably explain systemic market risk within this dataset.
